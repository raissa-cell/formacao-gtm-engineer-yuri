# Surface: Newsletter / Article Headline

ALSO read `references/text-style-rules.md` before generating.

## Cut point (D1 Fold Survival)
The headline IS the entire hook — there is no body to bail it out. It competes in a
crowded inbox/feed and on the social share card. Truncation hits around 60–70 chars on
share cards and search results, so the core promise must land inside ~60 chars. The
headline alone decides the open/click.

## Weights for scoring
D1 0.35 · D2 0.40 · D3 0.10 · D4 0.15
(Curiosity is the highest weight: a headline's only job is the click, and an unresolved
gap drives it. Pacing is low — a headline can carry one extra clause if it sharpens
the gap. Shareability matters for the share card but less than the click itself.)

## Length / structure
- 6–14 words, front-loaded promise.
- Specificity beats cleverness: a concrete number, outcome, or named tension out-clicks a pun.
- Avoid curiosity with no promise (pure clickbait erodes the newsletter's trust over time).
- The headline and any subject line for the same piece should differ (don't duplicate).

## Types that over-index
List Promise, Secret/Reveal, Contrarian/Myth-Bust, Statistic/Number, Contrast/Before-After.

## Worked examples
Topic: pricing strategy for B2B SaaS.
"The pricing mistake that's capping your ARR (and the 3-tier fix)"
(58 chars) D1 8 · D2 9 · D3 8 · D4 7 → 8.25/10. Named mistake + bounded promise + gap.

"We doubled revenue by raising prices. Here's the exact model."
(61 chars) D1 8 · D2 8 · D3 8 · D4 8 → 8.0/10. Counterintuitive result + promise of the asset.
