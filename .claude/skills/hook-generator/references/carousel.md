# Surface: Carousel (LinkedIn / Instagram document posts)

ALSO read `references/text-style-rules.md` before generating.

## Cut point (D1 Fold Survival)
The **cover slide** is the hook. It is text + visual, and its only job is to earn the
first swipe. Two failure modes: (1) the cover explains too much (no reason to swipe),
(2) the cover is pure curiosity with no promise of payoff (feels like clickbait, swipe-away).
The hook must promise a specific payoff AND withhold the answer.

## Weights for scoring
D1 0.35 · D2 0.30 · D3 0.10 · D4 0.25
(Pacing is low: the cover slide has visual support and a title can run slightly longer.
Shareability is high: carousels get SAVED and reshared, and saves are a ranking signal.)

## Length / structure
- Cover line: punchy title, 3–9 words. Often pairs with a visual cue or number.
- A subline (optional, smaller) can state the payoff: "7 slides. Steal them."
- Treat the cover as a title, not a sentence — fragments are fine.

## Surface mechanics for D4 (Shareability / Save)
A carousel that promises a reusable system, framework, or checklist gets saved. "Save
this" intent is the carousel growth loop. List Promise and "framework reveal" hooks win.

## Types that over-index
List Promise, Secret/Reveal, Contrast/Before-After, Statistic/Number.

## Worked examples
Topic: cold email that converts. Surface: Carousel.
Cover: "9 cold emails that booked $40k.  (swipe → the exact copy)"
D1 9 · D2 8 · D3 7 · D4 9 → 8.50/10. Number + proof + explicit save-worthy payoff.

Cover: "Your cold emails get ignored.  Here's the 3-line fix."
D1 8 · D2 8 · D3 8 · D4 8 → 8.0/10. Named pain + bounded promise that demands the swipe.
