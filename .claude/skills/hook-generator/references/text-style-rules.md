# Shared Text-Surface Style Rules

Apply to ALL non-video surfaces (LinkedIn, carousel, X/Twitter, text post,
newsletter/article headline, email subject). Video VO is exempt.

## Hard rules
- NEVER use em-dashes (— or --). Use a comma, period, colon, or line break instead.
- Line 1 of any hook is a STATEMENT, never a question opener and never a subordinate clause. (Exception: the Question hook type, where the question IS the line.)
- Language simple and conversational, minimally formal. Write as people speak.
- State stakes, numbers, and targets bluntly. No softening language.
- No hashtags unless the user asks. No emojis unless the user's own tone uses them naturally.
- Never write a gerund as the main verb construction (PT/ES: avoid "explorando", "aproveitando" as the verb spine).
- Never use reflexive formal constructions in PT/ES (e.g. "explorar-se", "observar-se").
- Short orphan lines are a feature: a single punchy line on its own row carries weight.

## Banned words (replace with direct, concrete alternatives)

**English:** crucial, essential, fundamental, key (adj), vital, journey, in summary,
in short, to summarize, basically, needless to say, it goes without saying, synergy,
leverage (verb), unlock, game-changer, seamless, robust, holistic, cutting-edge,
empower, disrupt.

**Spanish (Spain/Catalan bias):** crucial, esencial, fundamental, clave (adj), vital,
viaje / trayectoria (metafórico), en resumen, en definitiva, básicamente, huelga decir,
no hace falta decir, sinergia, aprovechar (corporativo), potenciar, transformador,
disruptivo, fluido, robusto, empoderar, desbloquear.

**Portuguese (Brazil):** crucial, essencial, fundamental, chave (adj), vital, jornada,
em resumo, em suma, basicamente, desnecessário dizer, sinergia, alavancar, desbloquear,
transformador, disruptivo, fluido, robusto, empoderar, potencializar.

## Tone (default)
Confident, direct, self-aware. Thought leadership with personality. Light humor allowed
via parenthetical asides (sparingly). POV, not a report. First person ("I") or direct
"you" — never indirect third person. Real examples and numbers beat vague metaphors.
